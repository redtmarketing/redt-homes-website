The 10 Most Walkable Neighborhoods in Denver 2025

Whether you’re ditching the car, downsizing your commute, or just love the idea of grabbing coffee, groceries, and tacos without ever starting your engine, Denver has no shortage of neighborhoods where walkability rules.

In this guide, we’re breaking down the 10 most walkable neighborhoods in Denver for 2025, based on local amenities, transit access, and that hard-to-define vibe that makes a place feel effortlessly livable.

What Makes a Neighborhood Walkable? 

A neighborhood isn’t just walkable because it has sidewalks. The best walkable communities have:

  • A mix of uses (housing, retail, restaurants, parks)

  • Transit connections to downtown or nearby hubs

  • A safe, people-friendly design

  • A vibe that makes you want to explore

And in Denver, they tend to come with breweries. Lots of breweries.

1. Capitol Hill; One of the Most Walkable Neighborhoods in Denver

Walk Score: 93
Cap Hill is the OG of Denver walkability. Think historic mansions turned apartments, dive bars next to vegan cafés, and live music every night of the week. From grocery stores to bookstores, everything’s a short stroll away.

Local Favorite: Cheesman Park sunsets and late-night eats at City O’ City.

2. LoDo: Best Downtown Denver Neighborhood for Walkability

Walk Score: 91
LoDo combines old Denver charm with modern urban energy. Historic warehouses now house rooftop bars, and Union Station is your portal to the airport, mountains, and beyond.

Local Favorite: Brunch at Snooze, Rockies games at Coors Field.

3. Highland (LoHi)

Walk Score: 88
Trendy and ever-evolving, LoHi is full of boutiques, rooftop patios, and walkable bridges into downtown. It’s urban living with a neighborhood soul.

Local Favorite: Happy hour at Avanti + skyline views from the pedestrian bridge.

4. Five Points / RiNo

Walk Score: 87
Street art, breweries, music, and old Denver soul. This neighborhood blends rich history with serious edge—and it’s super walkable. Expect lofts, light rail stops, and a lot of art.

Local Favorite: Morning coffee at Crema, murals on every corner.

5. Baker

Walk Score: 86
Eclectic and energetic, Baker is full of vintage shops, dive bars, and renovated bungalows. South Broadway is your walkable spine—lined with record stores, pho spots, and indie theaters.

Local Favorite: First Fridays on Santa Fe and a nightcap at Historians Ale House.

6. Congress Park

Walk Score: 84
Quiet, leafy streets just a stone’s throw from Cherry Creek and City Park. Congress Park blends classic Denver homes with walkable eats, libraries, and neighborhood bakeries.

Local Favorite: Pizza at Pizzeria Locale and movie nights at the Esquire.

7. Uptown

Walk Score: 83
Small but mighty, Uptown is packed with brunch spots, bars, and boutique apartments. It borders City Park and offers easy walkability to downtown and medical campuses.

Local Favorite: Outdoor seating at Ace Eat Serve and walks through Benedict Fountain Park.

8. Speer / Alamo Placita

Walk Score: 82
A hidden gem just south of downtown, Speer offers walkable living with a relaxed vibe. The Cherry Creek Trail runs right through it, connecting you to nature and city life.

Local Favorite: Farmers markets and patios along 6th Ave.

9. Wash Park (West Washington Park)

Walk Score: 81
Known for its iconic park, this neighborhood isn’t just for runners and dog walkers—it’s also full of cozy cafés, bookstores, and restaurants worth the stroll.

Local Favorite: Morning coffee at Wash Perk before a lap around the lake.

10. City Park West: Quiet Walkable Living Near Denver’s Top Attractions

Walk Score: 80
Quietly one of the best places for car-free living, City Park West offers walkability without the downtown intensity. Bonus: walkable access to City Park, the Denver Zoo, and Museum of Nature & Science.

Local Favorite: Brunch at Olive & Finch and strolls through the park.

🧭 Final Thoughts

Denver’s walkable neighborhoods offer more than just convenience—they’re hubs of culture, community, and connection. Whether you’re buying your first condo, relocating from out of state, or just ready to swap traffic for tacos, these 10 neighborhoods prove you can live the Denver dream—one step at a time.
