Townhome vs. Single-Family Home in Denver: Which Is the Better Buy?

← Back to Blog

By redT Homes Marketing · October 22, 2026 · Home Buyers Insights

Short answer: in Denver, a townhome is usually the better buy if you want a walkable, established neighborhood at a lower price with less maintenance. A single-family home is usually the better buy if you want more space, a private yard, no shared walls, and the long-term value of owning more land. If you want something in between, a paired home (half duplex) shares just one wall and often comes with its own yard.

Below we compare the two on price, monthly costs, maintenance, space, and resale, using current Denver market data, so you can decide which fits your life and budget.

Key takeaways

  • Price: the Denver metro median was $365,500 for attached homes (condos and townhomes) in September 2026, versus a $650,000 year-to-date median for detached homes, per DMAR.
  • Location: townhomes are often the most affordable way into close-in neighborhoods like Berkeley, Sunnyside, Platt Park, and City Park West.
  • Maintenance: townhomes usually mean less yard and exterior work. Single-family homes mean full control and full responsibility.
  • Monthly costs: check for HOA dues. Some townhome communities have none and use a party wall agreement instead.
  • Resale: single-family homes have historically appreciated faster because of land value. Today’s softer townhome market gives buyers more choice and negotiating room.

Price: what the Denver numbers say

According to the Denver Metro Association of Realtors September 2026 Market Trends Report, the median close price for attached homes was $365,500, down 6.28% from a year earlier, with 7.21 months of inventory. The year-to-date median for detached homes was $650,000, the same as in each of the past two years.

Two things to keep in mind when you read those numbers:

  • “Attached” includes condos. Older condos pull the median down. A new construction townhome in a central Denver neighborhood typically costs more than $365,500, but still less than a comparable new single-family home on the same street.
  • Townhome buyers have leverage right now. More than seven months of attached inventory is a buyer’s market, compared with 4.76 months across the whole Denver metro. That means more homes to choose from and more room to negotiate on price or incentives.

Monthly costs beyond the mortgage

HOA dues or party wall agreements

Many Denver townhome communities have an HOA that covers things like exterior maintenance, landscaping, snow removal, and sometimes insurance on the building. Dues vary widely, so ask for the budget and what’s included. Some smaller townhome communities skip the HOA and use a party wall agreement instead, which covers the shared walls between homes with little or no monthly cost. Single-family homes in Denver usually have no HOA.

Insurance

A single-family homeowner insures the whole structure. A townhome owner’s policy depends on what the HOA, if any, covers. Ask your insurance agent to quote the specific home before you write an offer.

Utilities

Shared walls mean less exposed exterior, so townhomes are often cheaper to heat and cool than a detached house of the same size. New construction of either type will typically beat an older home on energy costs.

Maintenance and time

With a single-family home, the roof, siding, yard, fence, driveway, and snow shoveling are all yours. That’s freedom and responsibility in equal measure. With a townhome, there’s less exterior and less yard, and depending on the community, some of it may be handled for you. If you travel often or would rather spend weekends on Tennyson Street than mowing, that matters.

Space, privacy, and lifestyle

Single-family homes win on square footage, storage, a private yard for kids or dogs, and no shared walls. Townhomes trade some of that for location: in Denver’s most walkable neighborhoods, a new townhome may be the only new construction option in your budget. Good townhome design closes the gap with features like rooftop decks, attached garages, en-suite bedrooms, and flexible lofts. Before you buy, ask how sound between units is handled. Our 11 questions to ask a Denver townhome builder covers what to look for.

Resale and long-term value

Land is what tends to appreciate, and a single-family home comes with more of it. That’s why detached homes have historically held value better through market cycles. Townhomes are more sensitive to supply, which is why prices have softened in 2026 as inventory has grown.

That doesn’t make a townhome a poor investment. A well-designed townhome in a walkable, in-demand neighborhood can hold its value well, and it can make a strong rental if your plans change. Location, layout, parking, and HOA costs matter more to resale than the property type alone.

The middle ground: paired homes

If you’re torn, look at paired homes (also called half duplexes). You share one wall instead of two, get windows on three sides, and usually have your own yard and garage, often without an HOA. Prices typically fall between a townhome and a detached home in the same area. Our Birch Leaf paired homes near Regis University are one example. See paired homes for sale in Denver.

Which is right for you?

A townhome may be the better fit if you:

  • Want to live in a walkable, close-in neighborhood near restaurants, parks, and downtown
  • Are buying your first home or downsizing
  • Prefer less yard work and exterior maintenance
  • Want new construction without the price of a new single-family home

A single-family home may be the better fit if you:

  • Need more space, storage, or a private yard
  • Want no shared walls and no HOA
  • Plan to stay long term and want the appreciation that comes with land
  • Want the freedom to add on, remodel, or landscape as you like

Frequently asked questions

Is a townhome or a single-family home the better buy in Denver?

It depends on your budget and priorities. A townhome usually gets you into a walkable, established Denver neighborhood for less money and less maintenance. A single-family home gives you more space, a private yard, no shared walls, and more land, which has historically driven long-term appreciation. Many buyers choose a townhome when location matters most and a single-family home when space and land matter most.

How much cheaper are townhomes than single-family homes in Denver?

According to the Denver Metro Association of Realtors, the median close price for attached homes (condos and townhomes) in the Denver metro was $365,500 in September 2026, while the year-to-date median for detached single-family homes was $650,000. New construction townhomes in central neighborhoods cost more than the attached median, but are still typically less than a new single-family home in the same neighborhood.

Do townhomes in Denver have HOA fees?

Many do, but not all. Some townhome communities use a party wall agreement instead of an HOA, which covers shared walls and sometimes shared items like snow removal, with little or no monthly dues. Several redT Homes townhome communities have no HOA dues.

Do townhomes appreciate as much as single-family homes in Denver?

Historically, single-family homes have tended to appreciate faster because more of their value is in land. In 2026, Denver metro attached prices fell about 6% year over year while detached prices held roughly flat, according to DMAR. That also means townhome buyers currently have more inventory to choose from and more room to negotiate.

What is a paired home?

A paired home, also called a duplex or half duplex, is two homes that share one wall. Each owner typically owns their own home and lot, with windows on three sides and often a private yard. It sits between a townhome and a single-family home in price, space, and privacy.
