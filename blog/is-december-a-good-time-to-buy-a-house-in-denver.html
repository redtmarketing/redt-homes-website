Is December a Good Time to Buy a House in Denver?

← Back to Blog

By redT Homes Marketing · November 10, 2026 · Home Buyers Insights

Short answer: for many buyers, yes. December is one of the quietest months for home shopping in Denver, so you'll face less competition, and sellers and builders who want to close before year-end are often more flexible on price, closing costs and rate buydowns. The catch is timing: the holidays shorten the closing calendar, so plan ahead.

Key takeaways

  • Less competition: fewer buyers are out over the holidays, so you have more time to decide and more room to negotiate.
  • Motivated sellers and builders: many want to close before December 31 and may offer price cuts, closing cost credits or rate buydowns.
  • Timing: a financed purchase usually takes 30 to 45 days, so contract by mid-to-late November for a December close.
  • Taxes: closing by December 31 may let you deduct that year's mortgage interest, points and property taxes if you itemize. Ask a tax professional.
  • Move-in ready homes are the safest bet for a year-end close.

Why December can work in your favor

Less competition

Between holiday travel, school breaks and the weather, a lot of buyers wait until spring. That means fewer people touring the same homes, fewer competing offers, and less pressure to waive inspections or decide on the spot.

Sellers and builders who want to close

A home that's still on the market in December often belongs to a seller who wants it sold before the new year. Builders are similar: finished homes cost money to carry, and many builders want to close them out before year-end. That's when you're most likely to see incentives like a mortgage rate buydown, help with closing costs, or upgrades included in the price.

Possible tax benefits

If you itemize deductions, the mortgage interest, discount points and property taxes you pay in the year you close may be deductible for that tax year. It doesn't help everyone, so talk to a tax professional before you time a purchase around it.

You see how the home handles winter

A December tour shows you things a June tour can't: whether the furnace keeps every room warm, where drafts come in, how the driveway and walkways handle snow and ice, and where water collects on the roof and gutters.

The trade-offs

  • Fewer new listings: inventory is usually thinner in winter, so you may have fewer resale homes to choose from. New construction helps here because builders keep selling year-round.
  • A shorter closing calendar: lenders, appraisers and title companies are often closed or short-staffed around Christmas and New Year's. Build in a few days of cushion.
  • Moving in the cold: book movers early, protect floors from snow and salt, and make sure utilities are transferred before move-in day.

Cutting it close? Ask whether the seller or builder will offer the same incentives for an early-January closing.

Frequently asked questions

Is December a good time to buy a house in Denver?

For many buyers, yes. Fewer people shop for homes in December, so there is usually less competition, and sellers and builders who want to close before year-end are often more willing to negotiate on price, closing costs or rate buydowns. The trade-offs are fewer new listings and a closing calendar shortened by the holidays.

Can I close on a house before the end of the year?

It depends on timing. A financed purchase typically takes 30 to 45 days from contract to closing, so a contract signed by mid-to-late November gives you the best chance of a December close. Move-in ready homes avoid construction delays, and title companies and lenders are often closed or short-staffed around December 24 to 26 and December 31 to January 1.

Are there tax benefits to buying a home before December 31?

Possibly. If you itemize deductions, mortgage interest, discount points, and property taxes you pay in the year you close may be deductible for that tax year. Whether it helps depends on your situation, so check with a tax professional before timing a purchase around taxes.

Do builders offer year-end incentives?

Many do. Builders often want to close finished homes before the end of the year, which can mean price reductions, closing cost credits, or mortgage rate buydowns on move-in ready homes. It never hurts to ask what incentives are available.

What should I check when touring a home in winter?

Winter is a good time to see how a home performs: check that the furnace heats every room evenly, look for drafts around windows and doors, see where snow and ice collect on the roof, gutters and driveway, and ask about insulation and energy efficiency ratings.
