Winter Energy Bills, Explained: What All-Electric Actually Costs in Denver

By redT Homes Team · January 15, 2026

If you've shopped for a home in Denver, you've probably heard that gas heat is "cheaper" than electric. Priced per therm versus per kilowatt-hour, that's often true. So why do all-electric, ENERGY STAR certified homes routinely end up with lower total winter bills than comparable gas-heated homes?

The building matters more than the fuel

A furnace burning cheap gas in a leaky, under-insulated house still loses most of that heat through walls, windows, and air gaps. redT homes are built with tighter envelopes and higher-performance windows, so there's simply less heat to replace in the first place — regardless of what's generating it.

Heat pumps do more with less

Modern electric heat pumps don't generate heat by burning fuel — they move existing heat, which makes them far more efficient than a standard furnace. For every unit of electricity in, a heat pump can deliver multiple units of heat out. That efficiency gap is what closes the price-per-unit difference between gas and electricity.

No separate gas bill, no separate gas maintenance

All-electric homes also skip the monthly gas service charge and the maintenance costs that come with a combustion appliance and venting system — costs that show up outside the "per unit" comparison but still hit your budget every year.

What this means for your monthly budget

Every home is different, and your actual bill depends on square footage, household size, and usage habits. But the pattern holds across redT's ENERGY STAR certified, all-electric homes: a tighter envelope plus efficient heat pump systems consistently beats the "gas is cheaper" assumption once you look at total cost, not just the rate on the bill.
